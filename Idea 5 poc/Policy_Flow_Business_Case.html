The Core Problem: Every enterprise chatbot built on an LLM (Gemini, GPT-4, etc.) has the same fatal flaw: it generates policy answers rather than retrieving them. For compliance-critical domains like KYC, DPDPA consent, HR benefits, or insurance claims — a hallucinated answer that miscommunicates a policy boundary is not just a UX failure. It's a regulatory violation, a legal liability, and a brand crisis.

📋 Real-World Hallucination Risks That Keep Compliance Teams Awake

("Bot told me I'm eligible for a loan") Scenario: A KYC chatbot powered by Gemini incorrectly tells a customer they qualify for a pre-approved loan based on a misinterpreted policy. Customer applies, gets rejected. Files SEBI grievance. Bank faces hearing.

("Bot confirmed I'm GDPR compliant") Scenario: An enterprise onboarding bot hallucinates a "yes" to "Does your product store data only in India?" when the actual policy requires EU customers to have data in Frankfurt. Regulator audit reveals the gap 18 months later. €500K fine.

The Solution
Policy-Compiled Flows — Zero Hallucination at Runtime

Policy-to-Flow Compiler uses Gemini once — at compile time — to translate enterprise policy documents into deterministic Dialogflow CX flow graphs. At runtime, the chatbot follows pre-compiled nodes. There is zero LLM generation at query time — only deterministic, policy-grounded responses drawn from the compiled flow.

The Architecture Insight: LLMs are powerful but non-deterministic. The innovation is moving LLM usage from "answer generation" (risky) to "policy compilation" (safe). The compiled flow is a legal and auditable artifact — as inspectable as a policy document itself. Every chatbot response traces back to a specific policy clause, enabling instant audit compliance.
