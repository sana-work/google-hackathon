Editing the template — ground rules
1. Replace every bracketed placeholder
Anything in [square brackets] is a placeholder. Nothing bracketed should make it to the final
version.
2. Show, don't tell - use screenshots
This is a proof-of-execution stage. Every team should have high-resolution, cropped
screenshots from inside the Fresco Play lab. Place them into the screenshot placeholders on
slides 6, 7 (architecture diagram), and 9 (build / deploy / validate).
4. Keep text short - for anything the audience must read
• Rule of thumb: no slide should have more than ~40 words of body text.
• If you find yourself writing paragraphs, move that content into your spoken delivery.
5. Quantify wherever possible
Slides 4 and 12 have placeholders like [XX%] and [SXX M]. Even rough, clearly-labelled
estimates (with your assumptions stated on the slide) are far stronger than vague claims.

How the template scores the 6-criterion rubric
The jury already used this rubric. Google's reviewers will look for the same evidence. The deck
is designed so each criterion has a dedicated home.
Novelty (15%) - slides 5 & 8
Move beyond prompt wrappers. Call out tool-use, sub-agent routing, multi-step reasoning,
webhook fulfillment. Screenshot the logic where possible.
Problem Fit (20%) - slides 4 & 5
Ground the solution in a specific persona and a specific bottleneck. Tie the elevator pitch
directly to the pain points on slide 4.
Implementation Feasibility (20%) - slides 7 & 9
Prove it was built and deployed. The three-panel Build / Deploy / Validate strip is specifically
designed to telegraph successful execution in seconds.
Long-term Value & Sustenance (20%) - slides 7 & 11
Show the architecture is modular and the solution is reusable. Use the Reuse Map to call out
adjacent accounts, industries, or workflows.
Security & Compliance (15%) - slide 10
Four named guardrails: PII, grounding, human-in-the-loop, domain boundaries. Replace each
bracketed description with your actual implementation detail — system instructions excerpt,
grounding source, HITL trigger, etc.
Evidence & Documentation (10%) - every slide
High-resolution, cropped, annotated screenshots. Captions on every image. Clean alignment. If
the jury cannot see it in a screenshot, they cannot grade it.

Common pitfalls to avoid
• Leaving [square bracket] placeholders in the final deck — do a find for T' before
submitting.
• Using real client names, logos, PII, or client documents. Anonymise or use synthetic
data — the hackathon rules require it.
• Low-resolution or uncropped screenshots. Recapture at native resolution. Trim
whitespace and sensitive UI.
• Reading the slides aloud. The audience can read. Use slides as evidence; speak to the
insight.
• Ignoring security. A deck without a credible Security slide drops a full band on the rubric.
Put real implementation detail on slide 10.
